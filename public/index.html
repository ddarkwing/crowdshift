MISSION

Why this matters

Global warming becomes operational risk

Heat is not only an environmental story. It becomes a crowd-management problem, a hydration problem, a medical-response problem and a timing problem. When a venue team sees only a weather warning, they still must translate it into action manually.

Europe needs practical heat resilience

In Europe, heatwaves increasingly affect cities, transport, public events and outdoor gatherings. CrowdShift focuses on the gap between forecast and response: what should the operator do, in what order, with what resources, before peak exposure arrives?

Prevention is the real KPI

The point is not to display a beautiful red map after the danger becomes obvious. The point is to detect rising risk early enough that teams can open cooling zones, add water, shift vulnerable activities and communicate clearly—so fewer people ever reach dangerous conditions.

ORIGIN STORY

Why we built this tool

CrowdShift was designed around a simple frustration: forecasts have improved, but operating teams still struggle to convert a heat signal into a clear response plan. They may know it will be hot, but not whether to open an indoor hall, add water, shift a children’s activity or reposition medical coverage first.

The product is intentionally built as a bridge between public climate data and local decision-making. Its purpose is not to replace authorities or emergency services. Its purpose is to make local action faster, clearer, more explainable and more preventive.

This is especially important in Europe, where heat can spread quickly across countries and where many cultural, urban and public events still need practical tools for resilience. CrowdShift turns climate stress into operational clarity.
